Geometric Time Score Estimators: Bridging Self-Predictive Representations and Generative Occupancy Models for Reinforcement Learning

Preprint
Aravind Venugopal¹, Grace Liu¹, Jeff Schneider¹, Benjamin Eysenbach²
¹ Carnegie Mellon University  ·  ² Princeton University
📄 Paper 💻 Code
Can occupancy density estimation along probability paths bridge the gap between self-predictive representations and generative occupancy models?

The time score \(\partial_k \log p_k(g^+ \mid s, a)\) captures the instantaneous rate of change of the log-density of the occupancy measure along a probability path. Integrating it gives accurate occupancy density estimates for downstream control.

Overview
  • Estimating the density of future outcomes under a policy's occupancy measure is central to RL. Self-predictive representations (CRL, FB, TD-JEPA, BYOL-\(\gamma\), ...) are the dominant approach, but we find they give surprisingly poor density estimates, even on simple MDPs.
  • Generative occupancy models (e.g. TD-flows) produce accurate samples of future states, but are not trained to estimate densities, so their expressivity goes unused for control.
  • We propose Geometric Time Score Estimators (GTSE): learn the time score of the occupancy measure along a probability path with temporal difference learning, and integrate it to recover the occupancy density.
  • Under a bilinear parameterization, the time-score integral decomposes into an infinite sum of inner products of self-predictive representations, which explains GTSE's higher representational capacity. A variant, GTSE-\(r\), learns explicit state-action and goal representations.
  • On OGBench locomotion and manipulation tasks, GTSE improves over state-of-the-art methods by 2× to 6× in goal-conditioned RL, and GTSE-\(r\) representations transfer to downstream reward-based RL.
What is an occupancy measure?

Start an agent at state \(s\) and take action \(a\). The occupancy measure is the distribution over the states \(s^+\) it visits in the future, with a state reached \(t\) steps later weighted by \(\gamma^t\):

\[ d^{\pi}(s^+ \mid s,a) = (1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t\, P(s_{t+1}=s^+ \mid s,a,\pi) \]

In goal-conditioned RL, \(d^{\pi}(g \mid s,a)\) equals the Q-value for reaching goal \(g\). Choosing the action that makes \(g\) most likely is choosing the best action, so a good density estimate is a good value estimate.

Below is the 5-step toy MDP from the paper, in \(\mathbb{R}^2\). Rollouts start at \(s\). After step 2, each one commits to one of two branches, which makes the occupancy measure multimodal. Each rollout adds weight \(\gamma^t\) to the heatmap when it reaches step \(t+1\). Move \(\gamma\) to shift weight between the near and far future. With \(\gamma \to 0\), only the next state is left: \(d^{\pi} = p(s' \mid s,a)\). Hover over the map to read the density at any goal.

Method
  1. Occupancy modeling along probability paths
    • The occupancy measure \(d^{\pi_D}(s^+ \mid s, a)\) is often a complex multimodal distribution. Instead of estimating it directly, we connect a simple base distribution \(p_0 = \mathcal{N}(0, I)\) to \(p_1(\cdot \mid s,a) \equiv d^{\pi_D}(\cdot \mid s,a)\) with a variance-preserving probability path: \[ s_k^+ = \alpha_k s^+ + \sqrt{1-\alpha_k^2}\, s_0^+, \qquad k \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1] \]
    • The time score \(\partial_k \log p_k(s^+ \mid s,a)\) is the rate of change of log-density along the path. Integrating it recovers the log occupancy density: \[ \log d^{\pi_D}(s^+ \mid s,a) = \int_0^1 \partial_k \log p_k(s^+ \mid s,a)\, dk + \log p_0(s^+) \]
    • Conditioning on the endpoint \(s^+\) gives a closed-form regression target \(\partial_k \log p_k(s_k^+ \mid s^+)\) for a network \(s_\theta(s_k^+, k, s, a)\).

  2. Geometric Time Score Matching (TD learning)
    • Monte Carlo targets cannot stitch trajectory segments. We use the recursive structure of the occupancy measure to bootstrap time-score predictions from the next state-action pair:
    \[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{GTSE}}(\theta) = (1-\gamma)\, {\color{#d4883e}\mathcal{L}_{\text{next}}(\theta)} + \gamma\, {\color{#641e16}\mathcal{L}_{\text{future}}(\theta)}, \qquad \forall (s,a,s',a') \in \mathcal{D} \] \[ {\color{#d4883e}\mathcal{L}_{\text{next}}(\theta)} = \mathbb{E}_{k,\, s'_k \mid s'}\Big[\lambda(k)\big(\partial_k \log p_k(s'_k \mid s') - s_\theta(s'_k, k, s, a)\big)^2\Big] \] \[ {\color{#641e16}\mathcal{L}_{\text{future}}(\theta)} = \mathbb{E}_{s^+ \sim d^{\pi_D}(\cdot \mid s',a')}\, \mathbb{E}_{k,\, s^+_k \mid s^+}\Big[\lambda(k)\big(\operatorname{sg}[s_{\bar\theta}(s^+_k, k, s', a')] - s_\theta(s^+_k, k, s, a)\big)^2\Big] \]
    • Next: noise the observed next state \(s'\) and regress on the closed-form conditional time score \(\partial_k \log p_k(s'_k \mid s') = \frac{\alpha_k\alpha_k'}{1-\alpha_k^2}\big(\dim(S) - \|\epsilon\|^2\big) + \frac{\alpha_k'}{\sqrt{1-\alpha_k^2}}\epsilon^\top s'\). On its own, this learns the \(\gamma \to 0\) occupancy: the next-state distribution.
    • Future: sample a future state from the model at \((s',a')\), noise it, and regress on the model's own (target-network) time score at \((s',a')\). This TD bootstrap stitches trajectory segments together.
    • At inference, the log-density of any \(s^+\) is a sum of \(N\) time-score evaluations: \(\log \hat{d}^{\pi_D}(s^+ \mid s,a) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} s_\theta(s^+, i/N, s, a) + \log p_0(s^+)\).

  3. Why it works: multiscale future prediction
    • If the time score factorizes as \(\phi(s,a,k)^\top \psi(g,k)\), the time-score integral has an exact Legendre decomposition: \[ \log d^{\pi_D}(g \mid s,a) = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} \hat{\phi}_l(s,a)^\top \hat{\psi}_l(g) + \log p_0(g) \]
    • Truncating at order \(L\) gives rank up to \((L+1)\delta\). Prior self-predictive methods learn rank-\(\delta\) approximations, which corresponds to \(L = 0\).
  4. GTSE-\(r\): explicit representations. Parameterizing \(s_\theta(g,k,s,a) = \phi_\theta(s,a)^\top \psi_\theta(g,k)\) gives \(\log \hat{d} \propto \phi_\theta(s,a)^\top \bar{\psi}_\theta(g)\): a state-action representation and a goal representation learned along probability paths.
Research questions

Each question below comes with its answer and the data behind it.

Q1.How well do existing methods estimate occupancy density?
Poorly, even on a 5-step MDP. On the toy MDP above, the best baselines (CRL, TMD, TD-flows) reach Spearman \(\rho = 0.78\) with the ground-truth \(\log d^{\pi_D}\). FB, TD-JEPA and Dual GR are near chance (0.19, 0.09, 0.19). CRL and TMD rank states near the origin well, where most of the mass is, but fail on far states.

Each panel plots the rank of a method's predicted density against the rank of the ground truth, over 50 goals × 300 state-action pairs. A perfect estimator puts every point on the red diagonal. Colour is the state's distance from the origin, so yellow means far. Click or hover a method to view its predictions.

Q2.Is GTSE a better estimator, and does that matter for control?
Yes, on both counts. GTSE reaches \(\rho = 0.96\) on the toy MDP and stays on the diagonal for far states (select GTSE above). On pointmaze-large-diverse, baselines degrade on far goals (\(\rho \le 0.30\)), and the best one (CRL) succeeds 3% of the time with best-of-16 action selection. GTSE ranks actions best for both near (\(\rho = 0.63\)) and far (\(\rho = 0.55\)) goals and succeeds 38% of the time, 12.5× CRL.

Q3.Where does GTSE's extra capacity come from?
From the higher-order Legendre terms. We train the bilinear variant GTSE-b and keep only the first \(L\) Legendre terms at inference (GTSE-b\(_L\)). \(L = 0\) has rank \(\delta\), like prior self-predictive methods. On the toy MDP, \(\rho\) rises from 0.82 at \(L=0\) to 0.96 at \(L=\infty\). On pointmaze, success rises from 12% to 25%. Even \(L = 0\) beats every baseline, so training along probability paths helps on its own.

Step through \(L\) to watch the far states (yellow) move onto the diagonal.

Q4.Does GTSE improve long-horizon goal-conditioned RL?
Yes, by 2× to 6×. Across 7 OGBench datasets, GTSE averages 37.9% success: 2× the strongest baseline (TD-JEPA, 18.6%) and 6× the weakest (TMD, 6.4%). GTSE-r is second (31.6%). GTSE is best on every manipulation dataset, including cube-triple. On antmaze, GTSE-r is ahead (78% vs 53%), likely because goal density there depends almost only on the agent's (x, y) position. All methods use best-of-16 action selection with the same flow-matching BC policy, so only the estimator differs.

Q5.Do GTSE-r representations transfer to reward-based RL?

scene-play

Yes. We freeze each method's representation encoder and train a Flow Q-Learning (FQL) critic on top of it, on the 5 reward-based scene-play tasks. Frozen GTSE-r features get the highest mean success, 62.2%, against 46.6% for TD-JEPA and 58.2% for vanilla FQL trained end-to-end. GTSE-r matches FQL on tasks 1 to 3, where both are near saturation. On task 4 it scores about 4× higher (36% vs 9%).
